2. PROBLEM AND RESULT WE CAN EXPLAIN

MODIS and VIIRS detect thermal activity with different resolution, response, and observation opportunities. Directly comparing their point counts can confuse an instrument change with a change in activity.

Our result maintains TWO lines per zone and week, on a common scale of adjusted reported counts. It does not convert each point into an independent fire. It also does not force the two curves to be identical.

The precise name to present this version is:
"Empirical harmonization of MODIS–VIIRS reported counts for Peru."

You can say: "We standardized the space, time, and relative response of the platforms, preserving missing data and uncertainty."

You should not say: "We reconstructed the true number of fires for all of Peru," "we corrected all historical cloud cover," or "we predicted where a fire will start."

3. DATA AND PREPARATION

Source: four NASA FIRMS ZIP files delivered by the team:
MODIS C6.1, VIIRS Suomi-NPP C2, NOAA-20 C2, and NOAA-21 C2.
MODIS includes the Terra and Aqua platforms; VIIRS is the instrument on the other three.

UTC dates, coordinates, platform, type, and quality variables were normalized.
Inventories, source rows, and hashes were preserved for traceability.
Scientific SP products and type=0, with presumably vegetation activity, are used.
NRT records whose type field is not available are not mixed automatically.
An identical confidence threshold was not imposed between instruments: their scales are different. The originals retain those fields for sensitivity analysis.

For the historical delivery, 2,228,157 detections from the five platforms were retained, after filtering by period, full weeks, type, and the official boundary of Peru.
They were aggregated into 855,553 positive cell-week-platform groups.

The boundary is a union of official MINAM departments, generalized for this analysis.
Its detail is approximately 500 m; two departments were refined to about 100 m.
The classification of points very close to the border may depend on that detail.

Why those dates:
- MODIS starts on the first full week of the archive: Monday, 2000-11-06.
- VIIRS begins on Monday, 2012-02-06, excluding the initial problematic period.
- The scientific common cutoff is July 2026. The week starting 2026-07-27 ends in August and does not fall entirely within the common cutoff.

4. SPATIAL AND TEMPORAL ORGANIZATION

An equal-area LAEA projection is used:
+proj=laea +lat_0=-9 +lon_0=-75 +datum=WGS84 +units=m +no_defs

Peru is divided into 13,405 10 × 10 km cells that intersect the country.
Border cells have less than 100 km² of surface area within Peru.

A cell-week is a fixed location observed during a fixed week.
Example: square A during the week of August 7 to 13.
The following Monday is another temporal unit of the same square.

The count is the number of points reported by a platform in that unit.
Presence of records only indicates if at least one exists: it is not the same as the number of detections, nor does it confirm the absence of fires when there are no points.

The R-tree served in the previous stage to verify geographic assignments.
The final preparation of the regular grid assigns points by projected coordinates and integer division; the proximity contrast uses a cKDTree spatial-temporal tree.
These are not algorithms that harmonize the instruments' sensitivity on their own.

5. HOW WE HARMONIZED THE COUNTS

Conceptual model:
expected count = shared activity × observational exposure × response.

The full national masks are not yet processed. Therefore, exposure cannot be entirely separated from the response: we calibrate an EMPIRICAL relative response, which can also absorb differences in observation opportunities.

During the overlap, we look for pairs of platforms with points in the same cell-week. Under a Poisson assumption, conditioning on the sum of their counts yields a binomial distribution that eliminates the shared activity.

Since the CSVs contain positives and not all observed zeros, the distribution is further conditioned on both platforms having detections. 1+1 pairs do not inform the contrast and are excluded.

The common reference is set by making the geometric mean of the Aqua and Suomi-NPP responses equal to 1. Neither is declared absolute truth.

Output formula:
comparable_count = reported_count / estimated_response
                 = reported_count × factor

Factors of the selected global model, approximately:
Aqua:       1.993784
Suomi-NPP:  0.501559
Terra:      4.311475
NOAA-20:    0.512807
NOAA-21:    0.520861

Example: 1 Aqua point produces 1.994 comparable units.
7 Suomi-NPP points produce 3.511 comparable units.
Those decimals express an estimation on the common scale, not fractions of a fire.
The remaining differences between instruments are possible and are not forcibly erased.

This version is NOT the previous VIIRS baseline converted to Aqua units.
Notebook 07 retains that exploratory baseline; notebook 09 and the current API use the symmetric common scale and the empirical-positive-pair-v1.1 revision.
The WAPE metrics from the previous baseline should not be attributed to the current model.

6. HOW WE AVOID ARTIFICIAL INCREASES FROM MORE SATELLITES

Each instrument uses one platform per week across the entire country:
- MODIS prioritizes Aqua; Terra allows extending the history prior to Aqua.
- VIIRS prioritizes Suomi-NPP; NOAA-20 serves as a bridge in a week without national Suomi-NPP records. NOAA-21 is calibrated as a bridge but it was not necessary to select it in the published main series.

Aqua+Terra or all VIIRS platforms are not summed to build each line.
Local gaps are also not filled by selectively adding other satellites.
Terra after 2023 is not used as a backup.

Published counts of selected platforms:
MODIS: 296,234 original detections; VIIRS: 1,025,785.
They are a subset of the 2,228,157 used in the multi-platform preparation.

513,756 positive cell-week-instrument rows are published.
The table by cell is sparse: it does not materialize millions of invented zeros.
The API generates the requested calendar and returns nulls where there is no estimation.

7. VALIDATION AND UNCERTAINTY

Global, seasonal, and regional-seasonal models were compared.
The selection uses 2019–2021 and requires at least a 1% likelihood improvement to increase complexity. The global one was kept: the other improvements were minor.

The adjustment of established platforms uses data up to 2021.
NOAA-21 enters with a bridge from March–June 2023 without changing previous factors.
200 km spatial blocks reserved for validation are excluded from the adjustment.
The final 2024–2025 test was opened after freezing the selection.
model.json retains a snapshot prior to evaluation; the executed result is credited in final_evaluation.json and final_test_2024_2025.csv.

Conditional MAE results, identity vs. calibration:
2024, seen zones:       1.833 -> 1.099
2024, reserved zones:   2.454 -> 1.261
2025, seen zones:       1.253 -> 0.971
2025, reserved zones:   1.328 -> 0.993

These errors measure the assignment of the observed total between two platforms in pairs with detections. They are NOT errors against the real number of fires.
MAE: mean absolute error; RMSE: heavily penalizes large errors.
Conditional NLL: measures fit to the observational distribution; lower is better.

95% bands: 100 bootstrap replicates with shared weights by 28-day blocks.
There are 237 training blocks and five in the NOAA-21 bridge.
In the national total, each complete replicate is summed, preserving its correlation.
The bands represent factor uncertainty under the model, not all uncertainty from missed fires, cloud cover, exposure, or the Poisson assumption.

IMPORTANT REVISION: v1.1 fixed a pandas temporal units error in the bootstrap. The previous bands were removed. The central parameters and forecast variables were preserved, verified to a tolerance of 1e-12. Regression tests were added for microsecond and nanosecond dates. Use the updated v1.1 package, not a previously downloaded copy.

8. CONTRAST WITH THE OFFICIAL MINAM REGISTRY

The 54,493 records of the official "Historical Registry of Fires 2000–2025" layer were downloaded and preserved by identifier.
Exact equivalence with the requested portal resource has not been demonstrated: the portal returned a 403, and we used the accessible official geographic service.

Dates, locations, duplicates, and INDECI/MINAM/ORORATECH origins were reviewed.
In the common period 2013–2025, 44,374 contrast references remained.
Not all equate to independent fires; some are references by location and date without a usable emergency code.

Fixed main window: 5 km and ±3 calendar days.
Sensitivities of 10 km/±3 days and 25 km/±7 days are reported.
Dates are also shifted −28 and +28 days as descriptive controls.

In references with a declared INDECI origin, the main match was:
MODIS: 19.64%; VIIRS: 45.11%.
When shifting dates ±28 days, those proportions were lower.
This provides evidence of temporal and spatial association, not a detection rate of all fires or a measurement of omissions. The independence of the registry regarding alerts and satellite images remains to be verified.

National weekly records were also correlated. Multiplying a series by a constant positive factor preserves the correlation: that correlation does not prove the harmonized scale is correct.

9. WHAT WE DID WITH THE ARTICLE

Equations of the published FRP operator were implemented independently:
https://doi.org/10.5194/gmd-19-10123-2026

Table constants, units, limits, and numerical behavior were verified.
A discrepancy was documented between table evaluations and summarized values in the text, with A0=0 as an explicit assumption pending confirmation.

The module remains research-oriented. It is not enabled as a production counts correction. FRP measures radiative power in MW; it is not the number of fires, and summing FRP from flyovers does not directly equate to emitted energy.

10. DELIVERED EXPERIMENTAL FORECAST

Target week fixed: November 16–22, 2026.
A row represents a 10 km cell.
record_probability estimates the probability of a weekly satellite record under the learned historical regime. It does not estimate the physical probability of ignition.

Variables: geography, cell fraction within Peru, seasonality, activity from approximately one year prior, and presence of records one and two years prior.
It does not use meteorology or recent detections from November 2026.
The comparable activity used as a variable is the average of available instruments, not their sum.

Training: 2013–2021.
Probability calibration: 2022.
Brier selection: 2023.
Final test: 2024–2025.
Selected model: HistGradientBoostingClassifier, against monthly climatology.
Training negatives are sampled at 12.5% with inverse weights; validation and test preserve their original distribution.

The spatial test reserves blocks; in those zones, previous history is available as a variable. It is not a test in locations without historical data.

Average precision measures ordering of positives; Brier measures probability error.
Examples in seen zones:
2024: AP 0.470; Brier 0.02897.
2025: AP 0.346; Brier 0.02536.

The model does not always outperform climatology. In zones seen during 2025, climatology achieves better AP and Brier; the result is preserved and documented.
No other model was selected after looking at the test.

The forecast is an experimental seasonal reference, not an operational alert.
The 2026 platform change is not yet validated in that forecast.
The map shows cell centers and probabilities, not ignition coordinates.

12. FIELDS YOU MUST KNOW HOW TO EXPLAIN

week_start_utc: Monday of the week; all dates in the series use UTC.
instrument: MODIS or VIIRS.
platform: satellite/platform selected that week.
raw_count: original points reported by that platform.
harmonized_count: adjusted count on the empirical common scale.
lower_bound/upper_bound: band of the calibration factor.
coverage_status=unknown: full observation coverage is not certified.
estimation_status=estimated: an adjusted count exists.
estimation_status=unavailable: no estimation is available; it does not mean zero.
method_version: method used, currently empirical-positive-pair-v1.1.
source_snapshot_id: identifies the inputs and the batch model.
calibration_period_status: warns of extrapolation prior to overlap or subsequent to validation periods.
record_probability: experimental probability of a record, between 0 and 1.
rank: experimental prioritization order, 1 corresponds to the highest value.

13. HOW TO RUN THE API TO DEMONSTRATE IT

Unzip the package into a folder. Open a terminal in that folder.
Tested with Python 3.13; a virtual environment is recommended.

python -m pip install -r api/requirements.txt
python -m uvicorn api:app --app-dir api --host 127.0.0.1 --port 8000

Open http://127.0.0.1:8000/docs

If you use a cloned Git copy instead of the ZIP package, first run:
python api/restaurar_snapshot.py
This reconstructs api/history.sqlite from data/history.sqlite.gz and verifies hashes and SQLite integrity. The delivery ZIPs already contain the uncompressed base.

Routes in the current delivery:
GET /v1/empirical/metadata
GET /v1/empirical/series?start=2000-11-06&end=2026-07-27&limit=1000
GET /v1/empirical/cells?limit=1000
GET /v1/forecasts?limit=100
GET /v1/validation

Add instrument=MODIS or instrument=VIIRS to choose a line.
Add cell_id for a cell. First discover valid IDs in /v1/empirical/cells.
end is EXCLUSIVE: to include up to July 26, use end=2026-07-27.
Follow pagination.next_offset until null: the maximum per page is 1,000.
Without instrument, the entire national history occupies 2,684 rows and requires three pages.
Bbox queries use west,south,east,north and filter cell centers.

IMPORTANT: /v1/series is reserved for the full correction, not yet published.
For this demo, use /v1/empirical/series.
It is a local API ready to integrate with HTML/JS, React, or Next.js. Public internet hosting is a separate deployment task.
